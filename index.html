Implicational generalizations in morphological syncretism

Introduction

Across languages, morphology often fails to mark grammatically relevant distinctions in some contexts. For instance, Danish marks the gender of third person pronouns in the singular, as shown in (1a), but fails to do so in the plural, as shown in (1b). In the plural, a single form is used, whether the referent iis a group containing only female individuals, male individuals or both.
